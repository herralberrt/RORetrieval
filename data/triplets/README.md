# BM25 triplets — dataset statistics

`(query, positive, hard negatives)` triplets for training a Romanian retriever.
The positive is not guessed: every query in `queries_gemma3_27b.jsonl` was
generated *from* a document, so that document is the positive by construction.
Retrieval is used only to mine negatives, and here it is **BM25**, not the
MiniLM bi-encoder of `triplets_27b.jsonl`.

This run also cleans the query set first, along the lines
[data/queries/README.md](../queries/README.md) argued for: the corpus is
deduplicated, boilerplate is removed, self-referential queries are dropped, and
six guards keep documents that answer the query out of the negatives.

## 1. Record format

```json
{
  "query_id": "q_0023626",
  "query": "Ce mărci de produse vestimentare sunt fabricate în România pentru grupul PVH?",
  "positive_doc_id": "zf_001121",
  "positive_source_doc_id": "zf_001121",
  "positive_title": "Cinci fabrici din România produc curele şi pantofi pentru Calvin Klein şi Tommy Hilfiger. …",
  "positive_bm25": 21.38,
  "positive_rank": 1,
  "additional_positive_doc_ids": [],
  "negative_doc_ids": ["zf_000116", "zf_001115", "zf_001907", "lib_000702"],
  "negative_similarities": [0.7872, 0.7498, 0.6081, 0.4077],
  "negative_bm25": [16.83, 16.03, 13.001, 8.716],
  "type": "news", "category": "zf",
  "duplicate_group": "58aec8aab45c",
  "query_version": "v1", "generator": "google/gemma-3-27b-it", "retriever": "bm25"
}
```

`negative_similarities` is the **score ratio** — the negative's BM25 score
divided by the positive's own score for the same query. It is what the hardness
band is expressed in, and it keeps the file readable by
`inspect_triplets.py`, which was written for the dense run. `positive_rank` is
where the positive itself landed in the BM25 candidate list;
`positive_source_doc_id` is the copy the query was generated from, before
duplicate documents were collapsed onto one canonical id.

## 2. Corpus preparation

| step | documents |
|---|---:|
| read from `data/categories/` (aggregate `news/` skipped) | 117 313 |
| after collapsing near-duplicate copies | 88 626 |
| after removing boilerplate and stubs | **87 170** |

Duplicates are grouped by **title + the first 600 characters**, not by a hash of
the whole text. Outlets republish an article with a different tail, which
changes an exact hash while leaving the same story; measured against the corpus,
the near-duplicate key collapses 753 groups that the exact hash misses, and
every merged group carrying more than one title turned out to be a syndicated
copy (`ade_*` and `dig_*` running the identical headline).

Boilerplate is cookie/consent banners, navigation stubs ("Știri Video actuale")
and anything under `--min-doc-chars` (300). Those documents are excluded from
the index entirely, so they can be neither positive nor negative.

## 4. How negatives are chosen

Candidates come from the BM25 top-100 within the positive's own document type.
A candidate becomes a negative only if it passes all of:

| guard | flag | default |
|---|---|---|
| scores below the positive (not a better answer) | `--max-neg-score-ratio` | 0.60 |
| scores above the easy floor | `--min-neg-score-ratio` | 0.15 |
| shares at least *n* of the query's scored terms | `--min-shared-query-terms` | 2 |
| covers a real share of the query's idf mass | `--min-query-idf-coverage` | 0.30 |
| contains at least one of the query's rarest terms | `--top-idf-terms` | 3 |
| is not the positive's own story (§9) | `--dup-idf-containment`, `--dup-token-overlap`, `--dup-title-overlap` | 0.45 / 0.7 / 0.6 |
| is not a near-copy of a negative already picked | `--dup-token-overlap`, `--dup-title-overlap` | 0.7 / 0.6 |

What each guard actually removed on this run:

| rejection reason | candidates |
|---|---:|
| fewer than 2 query terms in common | 699 184 |
| covers < 30% of the query's idf mass | 486 057 |
| outscores the positive (likely a better answer) | 293 594 |
| below the score band (`--min-neg-score-ratio`) | 200 207 |
| is the positive or an extra positive | 32 612 |
| **is the positive's own story → reclassified, not dropped** | **32 124** |
| contains none of the query's 3 rarest terms | 31 736 |
| near-copy of a negative already picked | 7 669 |

The same-story row is the one §9 added, and it behaves differently from the
others: those candidates are written out as positives with their provenance
rather than discarded.

The two idf guards exist because the score ratio is *relative*: when the
positive matches its own query poorly, unrelated documents look hard. The
`--max-positive-rank 5` filter closes the same hole from the other side — it
drops the query instead of accepting noise as negatives.

Mean 0.105:

| percentile | p5 | p25 | p50 | p75 | p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| lexical overlap | 0.026 | 0.053 | 0.085 | 0.137 | 0.255 |

## 6. Sanity check: can BM25 find the positive at all?

The positive is known by construction, so where BM25 ranks it measures the
*query*, not the retriever. Over the queries that reached the mining stage:

- of the queries that reached the mining stage: **72.1% at rank 1**, median
  rank 1, all within the top 5 (`--max-positive-rank 5` drops the rest)

That number is measured after the rank filter, so it describes the queries that
survived it, not the corpus. The 6 408 queries dropped for ranking below 5, plus
the 1 859 whose positive never appeared at all, are the honest denominator for
"how findable is a generated query's own document with BM25".

The overlap figures below were measured against the previous BM25 run and have
not been recomputed since; the argument they support is unchanged. Over the
21 173 queries both files contained, the mean overlap between their negative
sets was **0.052**, and **86.4% of those queries shared not a single negative
document**. The two sets are close to disjoint, which is the argument for
keeping both: lexical negatives punish term overlap without an answer, dense
negatives punish embedding proximity, and a model trained on their union sees
both failure modes. The dense file's negatives were also mined before the
cleaning described here, so it still contains the duplicate-query inflation.

## 8. Known limitations

1. **Residual false negatives.** §9 added a document-to-document guard and it
   moved 16 131 candidates out of the negatives, but that judgement is only
   ~70% precise and it runs in one direction: a same-story article that stays
   below the threshold is still a negative. No kept negative exceeds 0.5
   lexical overlap with its positive, which bounds how bad any single case can
   be, but it does not make the remainder clean.
2. **A few recipes are over-used as negatives.** The `recipes` pool is only 796
   documents, so the same recipe is a negative for up to 96 queries
   (`rec_000784`). Nothing similar happens in the other types (25 020 distinct
   documents are used as negatives overall).
3. **Hardness is relative.** `negative_similarities` compares against the
   positive's own score, so ratios are not comparable across queries with very
   different absolute scores. `negative_bm25` and `positive_bm25` are in the
   file for anyone who wants the absolute numbers.
4. **Yield.** 31.4% of the generated queries survive. 23 687 of the 47 909
   queries lost (49%) are the duplicate-query deduplication, which removes no
   information; the rest is the deliberate precision/recall trade of the guards.
   `MIN_NEGATIVES=1`, `MAX_POS_RANK=0` and `IDF_COVERAGE=0.2` recover most of it
   if volume matters more than precision for an experiment.
5. **No stemming.** The tokenizer folds diacritics but does not stem, so
   `Taiwanul` and `Taiwan` are different terms. Romanian is heavily inflected;
   a light stemmer would raise recall for both the positive and the negatives.
   §9 works around this for title comparison only.

## 9. What the 30 Aug review changed

Limitation 1 above turned out to be the real problem, and the review found a
clean instance of it. For *"Ce funcție ocupa Ion Rădoi la Metrorex, în perioada
controlului judiciar?"* the article kept as the hardest negative was headlined
*"Ion Rădoi poate redeveni lider de sindicat la Metrorex"* — which answers the
question. Three things were wrong:

1.  **The measure being read was the wrong one.** The `0.819` printed next to
    that negative is `negative_similarities`, i.e. BM25(negative, query) /
    BM25(positive, query) — how well it answers *the query*, relative to the
    positive. It is not an overlap between the two documents, so "96 articles
    above 0.8 lexical similarity" was counting something else. Nothing in the
    file measured document-to-document overlap at all. It does now, and
    `inspect_triplets.py` no longer labels the ratio "sim".
2.  **Every guard compared the candidate to the query, never to the positive.**
    Worse, `--min-query-idf-coverage` and `--top-idf-terms` *select for* the
    query's rarest terms — which for an entity question are the entity names.
    They actively promote other articles about the same person, which in a news
    corpus means the same story. No threshold on a query-relative ratio can fix
    that; only a document-to-document test can.
3.  **The one document-to-document test that existed could not fire.** Titles
    were compared as whole tokens, so `lider`/`liderul`, `sindicat`/
    `sindicatului` and `metrou`/`metrorex` all counted as misses: the pair above
    scored 0.30 against a 0.8 threshold.

The fixes, in `build_triplets_bm25.py`:

| change | flag | old → new |
|---|---|---|
| take negatives from lower down | `--max-neg-score-ratio` | 0.85 → **0.6** |
| pay for the lower ceiling | `--min-neg-score-ratio` | 0.25 → **0.15** |
| new: idf-weighted overlap with the positive **document** | `--dup-idf-containment` | — → **0.45** |
| compare titles on stems, not whole tokens | `--title-prefix` | — → **5** chars |
| title threshold, now that stems match more | `--dup-title-overlap` | 0.8 → **0.6** |

`--dup-idf-containment` weights each shared term by its idf, so sharing
`radoi`/`metrorex` counts for far more than sharing `declarat`/`instanta`.
Plain token overlap is useless here: any two Romanian news articles already
share a few hundred ordinary words.

A candidate that trips any of the three document-to-document tests is **not
dropped**. It is written out as a mined positive with the provenance of the
decision, so a reranker can be trained on the near-miss rather than losing it:

```json
"mined_positive_doc_ids": ["evz_000381"],
"mined_positive_provenance": [
  {"doc_id": "evz_000381", "source": "bm25_candidate",
   "reason": "title_overlap_with_positive", "rank": 3,
   "query_score_ratio": 0.6367, "bm25": 32.694,
   "lexical_sim_to_positive": 0.3906, "token_sim_to_positive": 0.4545,
   "title_sim_to_positive": 0.6}
]
```

Every kept negative carries the same fields in `negative_provenance`, so the
two populations can be compared directly.

The test runs on the reported case: `evz_000381` and `pro_000361` move to
`mined_positive_doc_ids`, while `evz_000070` — Rădoi asking for a sechestru to
be lifted, a genuinely different question — stays a negative at
`lexical_sim_to_positive` 0.25.

### Calibration run, and what it showed

The threshold was measured, not guessed: `git lfs pull` fetched the real 546 MB
corpus and the builder was run against it with `data/queries/sample_gemma3_27b.jsonl`
— 224 real queries, all four types, 117 313 documents → 87 170 indexed, 1.1 min
on 8 CPU cores. Two things came out of it, one useful and one that limits the
whole approach.

**The title rule was over-firing, and is now gated.** `title_overlap`
normalises by the *smaller* title, so a one-word title is a subset of every
title containing that word and scores 1.0 against all of them. `stories` is
full of them: "Poveste" vs "Poveste țărănească", "Fata moșului" vs "Fata
moșului cea cuminte" — different folk tales, sharing a name, with text overlap
of 0.065. 45 of the 81 title reclassifications were this. Two guards fix it:
`--min-title-terms 3` ignores degenerate titles, and `--min-lex-for-title 0.2`
means a title match can no longer reclassify a document whose *text* shares
nothing with the positive. That change is unambiguous — all 45 were errors.

**The idf measure does not separate "same story" from "same topic", at any
threshold.** This is the finding that matters. Ordered by `lexical_sim_to_positive`,
correct and incorrect reclassifications are interleaved end to end:

| lex | reclassified as positive | verdict |
|---:|---|---|
| 0.316 | *Mirel Rădoi va prelua CS Universitatea Craiova* ← question about his contract | right |
| 0.348 | *Două noi cutremure* ← question about a magnitude-4 quake | wrong, other quakes |
| 0.376 | *Înotătoarea care bătea recorduri la 50 m liber* ← question about Popovici | wrong, other swimmer |
| 0.403 | *Un nou cutremur de 3,2 în Vrancea* ← question about a magnitude-4 quake | wrong |
| 0.563 | *Simona Halep, de neoprit la Openul Canadei* ← question about that match | right |
| 0.928 | *Business Magazin: canicula persistentă* ← question about Australian fires | wrong |

Roughly **30% of reclassifications are wrong at every threshold**. Lowering the
cut does not buy precision, it just moves more of both populations. The reason
is structural: idf overlap sees shared *topic*, and what distinguishes two
earthquakes is the magnitude — a number — while the corpus carries no date
field to fall back on (`doc_id, title, text, summary, source, publication,
original_id`).

So `--dup-idf-containment` stays at the conservative **0.45**: fewer relabels
means fewer wrong ones in absolute terms. On the sample this yields 56 mined
positives over 20 of 76 triplets, with kept negatives at p95 0.255.

**Treat `mined_positive_doc_ids` as candidates for review, not as verified
positives.** At ~70% precision it is good reranker training material — which is
what it was asked for — but it is not ground truth. For that reason the
evaluation scripts count `positive_doc_id` and `additional_positive_doc_ids`
(both come from the generation prompt) as relevant, and deliberately **do not**
count the mined ones; doing so would inflate every metric with documents that
do not answer the query.

A real improvement would need an event-level signal rather than a better
threshold — the numbers in the query and the candidate are the obvious
candidate, since they are exactly what separates a magnitude 4 from a magnitude
3.2.

### What the rerun then measured

Everything above was predicted from the 224-query sample; the full run of 1 Sep
confirmed it.

* **The calibration holds at scale.** Kept negatives sit at p95 0.2549 over
  80 363 negatives, against 0.2551 on the sample — the threshold was not
  overfitted to 224 queries.
* **`too_easy` fell from 938 180 to 200 207**, as the counting-bug fix
  predicted. It is not comparable with the figure in §4.
* **Yield fell 34.3% → 31.4%**, all of it in `dropped_few_negatives` (8 662 →
  10 721). The deliberate price.
* **The sample had misled on source balance.** On 224 queries `alephnews` and
  `ro-stories` each looked like ~24% of the set; on the full run no source
  exceeds 10.1% and the distribution is far flatter (§10).
* **Split concentration was a small-sample artifact.** The worry that grouping
  by duplicate group would put one source in a whole split did not survive:
  the largest source is 11.4% of `eval` and 11.4% of `test`.

## 10. Training layout (`alina0195/ro-msmarco-divided` structure)

`triplets_27b_bm25.jsonl` is the working format — ids, scores, provenance. For
training it is flattened into the layout of
[`alina0195/ro-msmarco-divided`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/alina0195/ro-msmarco-divided),
which is three string columns holding the **text**, one row per
(query, positive, negative) pair — a query with four negatives becomes four
rows repeating the anchor and the positive:

| column | |
|---|---|
| `anchor` | the query |
| `positive` | the document it was generated from |
| `negative` | one mined hard negative |
| `query_source` | **added** — where the query comes from |

`query_source` is the upstream dataset or outlet of the positive: `adevarul`,
`zf`, `realitatea`, `readerbench/ro-stories`, `readerbench/ro-text-summarization
(alephnews)`, `BlackKakapo/recipes-ro`. The corpus is a merge of sources with
very different registers — news, folk tales, recipes, summarisation corpora —
and without the column an example carries no way to say which one it came from,
so it cannot be weighted, filtered or reported on per source.

```bash
python3 -m src.task2_triplets.export_hf_dataset \
    --input data/triplets/triplets_27b_bm25.jsonl \
    --output data/triplets/hf/ro_retrieval_triplets.jsonl
```

Writes `.jsonl`, plus `.parquet` when pyarrow is available (it is, inside
`containers/roretrieval.sif`). Two deliberate differences from a naive dump:

* **No `Titlu:` / `Text:` labels.** Those are prompt scaffolding for the query
  generator, not corpus content; training on them teaches a pattern no real
  query carries. Title and body are joined with a blank line instead.
* **`--split` groups by duplicate group.** A row-level random split would put a
  query in train and a re-published copy of its positive in test, inflating
  exactly what the split is meant to measure. Verified: zero positives shared
  across splits.

### Plot and upload

The whole chain runs in one job — `scripts/slurm/build_triplets_bm25.sbatch`
builds the triplets, exports the training layout, and plots `query_source`.
`EXPORT=0` skips the last two, `SPLIT=1` writes train/eval/test:

```bash
SPLIT=1 sbatch --partition=haswell scripts/slurm/build_triplets_bm25.sbatch
```

The plot has two panels because rows and queries answer different questions:
rows are what a training epoch actually sees (a query with four negatives is
four rows), queries are how much of each source the generator covered. A source
can be large in one and small in the other.

> On a small set, grouping the split by duplicate group concentrates sources —
> on the 224-query sample the eval split came out 100% `alephnews` and test 100%
> `mediafax`. With thousands of groups this disappears, but check the per-source
> breakdown the exporter prints before trusting a split.

Uploading is a separate, deliberately awkward step — it needs a token, and
`--yes` on top of `--public`, because a public dataset is not really
retractable:

```bash
HF_TOKEN=hf_... apptainer exec containers/roretrieval.sif \
    python3 -m src.task2_triplets.push_to_hf \
        --input data/triplets/hf/triplets_27b_bm25_hf_train.jsonl \
        --repo PaulBurca2005/ro-retrieval-triplets --public --yes
```

`--card-only` prints the dataset card without uploading. The card carries the
limitations from §8 and §9 — in particular that the mined positives are ~70%
precise — rather than leaving someone downstream to discover them.
